A Step-by-Step Batch-Resize Workflow for Online Sellers

A repeatable process for turning a folder of raw product photos into listing-ready images for Etsy, Shopify, Amazon, or any marketplace — without opening a single file by hand.

Install Extension

Most sellers already know that they should resize product photos consistently — mismatched dimensions make a storefront look inconsistent, and some marketplaces reject or auto-crop images that don't match their spec. What's missing more often is the process: a fixed order of steps you can run every time a new batch of product photos comes in, instead of re-deciding how to handle images listing by listing.

Below is a six-step workflow built around batch processing in Bulk Image Resizer — a Chrome extension that resizes and converts images directly in your browser, with no upload step. It assumes you're starting with a folder of raw photos (from a phone, a camera, or a supplier) and ending with a folder of listing-ready files.

The Six-Step Workflow

1

Collect everything into one folder first

Before resizing anything, get every photo for the batch — a single product's shots, or a whole catalog update — into one folder. Mixing in only-half-the-batch now means running the resize step twice later.

2

Cull obvious rejects before you resize

Delete blurry, duplicate, or clearly unusable shots now. Resizing is fast either way, but there's no reason to batch-process photos you already know won't make the listing.

3

Decide on one target size (or one preset set) for this batch

Check your marketplace's current image requirements — Etsy, Shopify, and Amazon each publish recommended listing-image dimensions, and they're not identical. Settle on the width and height for this batch before you open the extension, so you're not second-guessing mid-batch.

4

Open the whole folder as one batch in Bulk Image Resizer

Rather than selecting files one at a time, upload the entire folder. The extension treats every image in it as a single batch, so the resize settings you choose next apply uniformly to all of them.

5

Set custom dimensions (or a preset) and pick your output format

Enter the exact width and height your storefront requires, or select a smart preset if one matches. Choose JPG, PNG, or WebP as the output format in the same step — there's no need for a separate conversion pass afterward.

6

Process the batch and spot-check before uploading to your listings

Run the batch — hundreds of images resize in seconds, processed locally in your browser. Open a handful of the output files to confirm crop and quality look right, then upload the finished set to your listings.

Where This Saves the Most Time

Catalog-wide re-sizes

When a marketplace changes its recommended dimensions, or you switch storefronts, you can re-run an entire existing product catalog through new settings in one batch instead of re-editing each listing's photos individually.

Multi-marketplace sellers

Selling the same products on Etsy, Shopify, and Amazon often means producing slightly different image dimensions per platform. Running the same source folder through the extension multiple times, once per target size, is still far faster than manual editing.

Supplier photo cleanup

Supplier-provided product photos rarely arrive pre-sized for your storefront. A batch pass standardizes dimensions and format before anything goes live, without touching each file.

Unreleased inventory stays private

Because processing happens locally in your browser rather than on a server, photos of inventory that hasn't launched yet aren't uploaded anywhere during this step.

A Note on Formats

JPG remains the safest default for most marketplace listing photos — it's universally supported and keeps file sizes reasonable. WebP produces smaller files at comparable quality and is worth using for a storefront you control directly (like a Shopify theme), but double-check that the specific marketplace you're listing on actually accepts it before switching your whole catalog over. PNG is the right call when a product shot needs a transparent background, such as a cut-out product image for a listing template.

Common Mistakes That Slow This Down

Resizing before culling. Running a batch resize on photos you're about to delete anyway wastes a step. Cull first, resize second — it's a small reordering that adds up across dozens of batches a year.

Guessing at dimensions instead of checking current requirements. Marketplaces update their recommended image sizes more often than sellers expect. A dimension that was correct a year ago may now get your image padded, cropped, or flagged. Check the current spec for your specific marketplace before setting a batch's target size, rather than reusing whatever worked last time.

Mixing portrait and landscape source photos in one batch without checking the crop. If your target size has a fixed aspect ratio and your source photos don't share one, resizing can crop more aggressively on some images than others. A quick spot-check after the batch finishes catches this before it reaches a live listing.

Treating every marketplace's image requirements as identical. It's tempting to resize once and reuse the same files everywhere, but Etsy, Shopify, and Amazon don't share identical recommended dimensions or aspect ratios. Running the same source folder through a second batch pass with different target dimensions is still faster than editing by hand, and it avoids a photo looking slightly off on one of your sales channels.

Making the Workflow Stick

The value of a fixed six-step process isn't any single step — it's that you stop re-deciding how to handle images every time a new batch of product photos lands. Once you've settled on target dimensions per marketplace and an output format per use case, those choices don't need to be revisited for every new catalog update; only the source folder changes. If you find yourself making the same resize decision over and over, that's usually a sign it's worth writing down as a fixed setting for next time, rather than re-evaluating it on every batch.
